Product Demo Execution
Demo Script Structure (15 minutes)

0-2 min: Context Setting

"Based on our conversation, here is what I will show you: how our product helps cloud security leads at mid-size fintech companies fix critical exposures first."

A question to open with, in this sector's language: "Which assets, systems or environments are in scope, and which are you least sure about?"

2-8 min: Core Value Demonstration
Start with what sets our product apart. In one line, the difference: Our product offers our product ranks every misconfiguration by real exposure so the team fixes what matters first. Then show the two or three features that answer the buyer's stated needs. Keep the order of what the buyer measures: critical exposures open.

8-12 min: Differentiation Proof

"You might be wondering how this compares to what you use today. Let me show you."

Show one thing they cannot get from their current approach, using their own data or sites where you can.

12-15 min: Close and Next Steps

"What would success look like for you in the first 90 days?"
"The next step is a time-boxed proof of value."
Best Practices
  • Customise to their specific use case.
  • Use their industry terms (attack surface, exposure, misconfiguration, alert fatigue).
  • Show outcomes, not features.
  • Leave time for questions and agree the next step before the end.
Sector Language

Use the words buyers in cybersecurity, cloud security use, and keep claims to what you can show. Words this sector's buyers use: attack surface, exposure, misconfiguration, alert fatigue, mean time to detect, mean time to respond, SOC, compliance audit, risk register, threat intelligence. What the sector measures: critical exposures open. A proof point that lands: Exposures found and closed during the proof of value, with the time it took to fix them.

Channel Priority

The order follows how deals usually run in cybersecurity, cloud security: CISO led, with a proof of value on the buyer's own environment; budget often follows an audit finding or an incident.

Recommended Priority Order:

  1. Cold Email (direct outreach to the roles that buy: CISO; SOC or cloud security lead)
  2. LinkedIn Organic (awareness among those same roles)
  3. Sales Deck (the story you tell once there is a meeting)
  4. Website (credibility: proof, references and the facts buyers check)
  5. Demo (proof of capability)

To sharpen this, give: the alternative buyers use today, written in the statement as "Unlike X" or "Alternatives buyers use today: X" (it would change the comparison lines on the page, in the posts, the deck and the demo); a customer count, a result or a recognition with its source (for example in target customer or key benefit) (it would change the proof strip, the emails and the results slide); how you charge (it would change the calls to action and the commercial terms).

How to read the answer

The tool translates your Positioning statement, Target customer, and Key benefit into a Channel Execution Playbook covering Website Execution (hero headline, subheadline, proof strip, and calls to action), LinkedIn Execution (tagline and 3 post drafts), Cold Email Execution (3-email sequence), Sales Deck Execution (10-slide structure and differentiation slide), a 15-minute Product Demo Execution script, and Channel Priority. If you did not type a customer count, result, or recognition with its source, the Proof strip is left blank and Post 3 prints an outline rather than a fabricated story.

What to do next

  • Founder: Check Website Execution and Sales Deck Execution to confirm that the homepage hero and Slide 1 carry the exact same core benefit and buyer role.
  • CEO: Review Channel Priority to ensure the team invests in the channels that match how deals actually run in your sector (for example, direct cold email and LinkedIn to the CISO and security lead before broad awareness).
  • CMO: Add the alternative buyers use today ("Unlike X"), a verified customer result with its source, and how you charge (as listed under To sharpen this), then re-run the tool to fill the proof strip and comparison lines.
  • CRO: Roll out the 10-slide Sales Deck Execution structure and the 15-minute Product Demo Execution script so reps show differentiation on the buyer's own data by minute 8.
  • Demand generation head: Personalize the 3-email Cold Email Execution sequence by opening Email 1 with a visible buyer trigger (such as an audit, renewal, or hiring change) and publish Post 1 and Post 2 on LinkedIn.

Who is the Translate Execution tool not ideal for?

  • Anyone who cannot give the inputs listed above, because the answer is built only from them.
  • Teams that need buyer research done for them: IMPACT structures your positioning as hypotheses, and you test them with buyers.
  • Anyone who needs live competitor data: the tools do not look anything up. They work from what you type about your market and rivals, and anything they add themselves (such as common complaints in a category) is marked as something to check with buyers.

How does the Translate Execution tool work?
