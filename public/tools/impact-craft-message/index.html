Level 3: Three Supporting Pillars

Pillar 1: the result. Unproved value hypothesis: What buyers get: fewer critical cloud exposures.

  • Proof you have: none given for the result yet.
  • Proof to collect: exposures found and closed during the proof of value, with the time it took to fix them.

Pillar 2: the difference. What sets it apart: Our product offers exposure-based ranking of every misconfiguration.

  • Proof you have: none given for the difference yet.
  • Proof to collect: a side by side with periodic manual cloud audits on the same customer data, for critical exposures open and alerts per analyst.

Pillar 3: why it is safe to buy. Buyers in this sector often raise "We already have a tool for this"; the answer is under Objection Handling.

  • Proof you have: no customer count or reference given yet.
  • Proof to collect: a reference or pilot result that answers that objection.

Words this sector's buyers use, to check your wording against: attack surface, exposure, misconfiguration, alert fatigue, mean time to detect, mean time to respond, SOC, compliance audit, risk register, threat intelligence.
What buyers in a business like yours also weigh: term, scope and price.

Audience-Specific Messaging

For the champion (SOC or cloud security lead):

Our product gives you a way to get fewer critical cloud exposures, instead of living with periodic manual cloud audits.

For the economic buyer (CISO):

Our product lets your team get fewer critical cloud exposures. Measure it in critical exposures open and alerts per analyst, the figures this sector already watches. They will also weigh term, scope and price.

For the technical evaluator (risk, compliance and audit):

On risk, in your words: "cloud security monitoring platform". For the technical review: Our product offers exposure-based ranking of every misconfiguration. Our product is a cloud security monitoring platform.
Objection Handling
  • "We already use periodic manual cloud audits." Acknowledge it, then ask where it leaves cloud security leads at mid-size fintech companies short. Then show the difference: Our product offers exposure-based ranking of every misconfiguration.
  • "The price is too high." Tie the price to critical exposures open, measured in the buyer's own data, and agree how the cost will be compared (term, scope and price).
  • "We already have a tool for this." Map the overlap honestly and show what the current tool misses, on the buyer's own environment.
  • "We have too many alerts already." Show how findings are ranked by real exposure so the team works on fewer, more important items.
  • "Integration with our SIEM and ticketing." Name the integrations available today and test them in the proof of value.
  • "We need proof before budget." Offer a fixed length proof of value with success criteria agreed in writing first.
Message Tests
  • Show the headline to someone outside the company for five seconds and ask what our product does.
  • Ask a customer to confirm in their own words the result our product gives them, and use their words.
  • Check every line against the words this sector's buyers use (attack surface, exposure, misconfiguration, alert fatigue, mean time to detect).

To sharpen this, give: a customer count or a result with its source typed into key benefit or differentiation (it would change the proof lines of the three pillars and the proof-led variation); how you charge (it would change the cost, call to action and proof wording).

Who is Craft Message not ideal for?

  • Anyone who cannot give the inputs listed above; the answer is built only from them.
  • Teams that need buyer research done for them: IMPACT structures your positioning as hypotheses, and you test them with buyers.
  • Anyone who needs live competitor data: the tools look nothing up; they work from what you type about your market and rivals, and anything they add themselves (such as common complaints in a category) is marked as something to check with buyers.

How do I use Craft Message with an AI assistant?

Add IMPACT once (see Connect your app), then ask in plain words. A prompt built from the tested example:

My role is not stated. Ask me for my role first. Use IMPACT's Craft Message tool. product name: our product; target customer: cloud security leads at mid-size fintech companies; customer need: lose days chasing low-risk cloud alerts; product category: cloud security monitoring platform; key benefit: Fewer critical cloud exposures; competitor: periodic manual cloud audits; differentiation: Exposure-based ranking of every misconfiguration. If this tool is unavailable, ask me to open its web page; do not invent its output. In Gemini CLI or Antigravity, use the Helix GTM Suite skills package for related guidance, without claiming to run this connector.

How does Craft Message work?

No sign-in. A web browser is enough, and the answer comes back in a second or two.

  1. Fill in the form above, or press Fill in an example.
  2. Press Run Craft Message.
  3. Read the result on this page, then copy it and edit it before you use it.

It applies fixed rules in the IMPACT code to the inputs above and returns text. It does not call an AI model, does not look anything up, and does not store what you type. The web form and the AI assistant connector run the same tool code, so the same input gives the same answer in both.
