Craft Message

Build your positioning statement and message hierarchy

For CMOs and marketing teams. The decision: what is our core message, and how do we test it?

Give it your target customer, key benefit and what sets you apart, and you get a positioning statement in three lengths, a message hierarchy, versions to test and answers to objections.

Last updated 5 November 2026

Would our B2B buyer choose this positioning claim, and how do we test it with buyers?

Build a positioning statement, tagline options, three supporting pillars, audience-specific copy (champion, economic buyer, and technical evaluator), and objection answers from your target customer, key benefit, and differentiation. Every benefit is marked as an unproved value hypothesis until backed by measured proof, with a five-second headline clarity check in Craft Message.

Level 3: Three Supporting Pillars

Pillar 1: the result. Unproved value hypothesis: What buyers get: fewer critical cloud exposures.

  • Proof you have: none given for the result yet.
  • Proof to collect: exposures found and closed during the proof of value, with the time it took to fix them.

Pillar 2: the difference. What sets it apart: Our product offers exposure-based ranking of every misconfiguration.

  • Proof you have: none given for the difference yet.
  • Proof to collect: a side by side with periodic manual cloud audits on the same customer data, for critical exposures open and alerts per analyst.

Pillar 3: why it is safe to buy. Buyers in this sector often raise "We already have a tool for this"; the answer is under Objection Handling.

  • Proof you have: no customer count or reference given yet.
  • Proof to collect: a reference or pilot result that answers that objection.

Words this sector's buyers use, to check your wording against: attack surface, exposure, misconfiguration, alert fatigue, mean time to detect, mean time to respond, SOC, compliance audit, risk register, threat intelligence.
What buyers in a business like yours also weigh: term, scope and price.

Message Variations

Variation A: lead with the problem

Unproved value hypothesis: Do you lose days chasing low-risk cloud alerts? The difference in one line: Our product offers exposure-based ranking of every misconfiguration.

Variation B: lead with the outcome

Unproved value hypothesis: Our product: Fewer critical cloud exposures. Built for cloud security leads at mid-size fintech companies.

Variation C: lead with the difference

Variation D: lead with social proof

No customer count, result or recognition was typed, so there is no honest proof line to write yet. The first pillar says what to collect.
Audience-Specific Messaging

For the champion (SOC or cloud security lead):

Our product gives you a way to get fewer critical cloud exposures, instead of living with periodic manual cloud audits.

For the economic buyer (CISO):

Our product lets your team get fewer critical cloud exposures. Measure it in critical exposures open and alerts per analyst, the figures this sector already watches. They will also weigh term, scope and price.

For the technical evaluator (risk, compliance and audit):

On risk, in your words: "cloud security monitoring platform". For the technical review: Our product offers exposure-based ranking of every misconfiguration. Our product is a cloud security monitoring platform.
Objection Handling
  • "We already use periodic manual cloud audits." Acknowledge it, then ask where it leaves cloud security leads at mid-size fintech companies short. Then show the difference: Our product offers exposure-based ranking of every misconfiguration.
  • "The price is too high." Tie the price to critical exposures open, measured in the buyer's own data, and agree how the cost will be compared (term, scope and price).
  • "We already have a tool for this." Map the overlap honestly and show what the current tool misses, on the buyer's own environment.
  • "We have too many alerts already." Show how findings are ranked by real exposure so the team works on fewer, more important items.
  • "Integration with our SIEM and ticketing." Name the integrations available today and test them in the proof of value.
  • "We need proof before budget." Offer a fixed length proof of value with success criteria agreed in writing first.
Message Tests
  • Show the headline to someone outside the company for five seconds and ask what our product does.
  • Ask a customer to confirm in their own words the result our product gives them, and use their words.
  • Check every line against the words this sector's buyers use (attack surface, exposure, misconfiguration, alert fatigue, mean time to detect).

To sharpen this, give: a customer count or a result with its source typed into key benefit or differentiation (it would change the proof lines of the three pillars and the proof-led variation); how you charge (it would change the cost, call to action and proof wording).

How to read the answer

The tool turns your Target customer, Key benefit, and Differentiation (plus optional Product name, Customer need, Product category, and Competitor) into a Positioning Statement (Complete and One-Sentence Version), a three-level Message Hierarchy (Tagline, Value Proposition, and Three Supporting Pillars), Message Variations (A to D plus Audience-Specific Messaging), Objection Handling, and Message Tests. If you did not include a measured customer result with its source and period, every benefit line is prefixed with Unproved value hypothesis: and Variation D: lead with social proof prints no proof line until you collect the evidence described under Three Supporting Pillars.

What to do next

  • Founder: Follow Confirm one claim and its proof: pick one outcome claim, attach a measured customer result with its period and source, and get written permission before publishing it.
  • CEO: Run the five-second headline test under Message Tests with someone outside your company to verify they can state what your product does and for whom.
  • CMO: Collect the missing evidence listed under Pillar 1, Pillar 2, and Pillar 3 (a before-and-after pilot result, a side-by-side comparison on the same customer data, and a customer reference) and re-run the tool.
  • CRO: Give reps the Audience-Specific Messaging (champion, economic buyer, and technical evaluator) and the six Objection Handling responses for active deals.
  • Demand generation head: Test Variation A (problem-led), Variation B (outcome-led), and Variation C (difference-led) in outbound and social campaigns once the value hypothesis is backed by proof.

Why come back

Come back once you have a verified customer count or before-and-after result with its source, and re-run the tool with your billing model specified so the proof lines and commercial calls to action are complete. The tool applies the same fixed rules every time. It does not call an AI model, and this site does not keep what you enter.

Who is the Craft Message tool not ideal for?

  • Anyone who cannot give the inputs listed above, because the answer is built only from them.
  • Teams that need buyer research done for them: IMPACT structures your positioning as hypotheses, and you test them with buyers.
  • Anyone who needs live competitor data: the tools do not look anything up. They work from what you type about your market and rivals, and anything they add themselves (such as common complaints in a category) is marked as something to check with buyers.

How does the Craft Message tool work?

You do not need to sign in. A web browser is enough, and the answer comes back in a second or two.

  1. Fill in the form above, or press Fill in an example.
  2. Press Run Craft Message.
  3. Read the result on this page, then copy it and edit it before you use it.

It applies fixed rules in the IMPACT code to the inputs above and returns text. It does not call an AI model, does not look anything up, and does not store what you type. The web form and the AI assistant connector run the same tool code, so the same input gives the same answer in both.
