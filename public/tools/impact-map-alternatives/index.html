Status Quo (Current Manual/DIY Approach)

What they may be doing instead (common patterns for this kind of seller; check them with buyers):

  • The current security tools plus manual review by analysts
  • A periodic scan or audit
  • Doing nothing until an incident or an audit finding

Buyers in this sector often say: "We already have a tool for this" and "We have too many alerts already". The status quo persists while changing costs more effort or budget than the problem costs today, so put the cost of the current approach in the buyer's own figures (mean time to detect, mean time to respond, critical exposures open) and tie the change to a dated event such as a renewal, an audit, a season or a target.

Against Do Nothing

Not buying is the alternative every deal faces: it wins when the problem does not yet cost the buyer enough to act. Ask what it costs in the buyer's own figures, for example mean time to detect or mean time to respond, and what changes if it grows next year.

Discovery Questions for Competitive Intel

Ask prospects these questions to understand their competitive context:

  1. "What have you tried before to solve this?"
  2. "What other solutions are you evaluating?"
  3. "What keeps you with your first competitor today, and what would make you change?"
  4. "What keeps you with your second competitor today, and what would make you change?"
  5. "What didn't work about your previous approach?"
  6. "What's missing from solutions you've seen?"
Sector view: cybersecurity, cloud security
  • Words this sector's buyers use: attack surface, exposure, misconfiguration, alert fatigue, mean time to detect, mean time to respond, SOC, compliance audit, risk register, threat intelligence.
  • Who usually buys: The CISO signs; the SOC or cloud security lead champions; security engineers use it; risk, compliance and audit review the evidence it produces; the CIO or CTO checks integration.
  • Objections this sector often raises: "We already have a tool for this"; "We have too many alerts already"; "Integration with our SIEM and ticketing"; "We need proof before budget".
  • Discovery questions in this sector's language:
  1. Which assets, systems or environments are in scope, and which are you least sure about?
  2. How many alerts does the team handle in a week, and how are they ranked today?
  3. What did the last audit or incident show?
  4. Which tools must this work with (SIEM, ticketing, cloud accounts)?
  5. How would you judge a proof of value a success?

Who is the Map Alternatives tool not ideal for?

  • Anyone who cannot give the inputs listed above, because the answer is built only from them.
  • Teams that need buyer research done for them: IMPACT structures your positioning as hypotheses, and you test them with buyers.
  • Anyone who needs live competitor data: the tools do not look anything up. They work from what you type about your market and rivals, and anything they add themselves (such as common complaints in a category) is marked as something to check with buyers.

How do I use the Map Alternatives tool with an AI assistant?

Add IMPACT once (see Use it in Claude or ChatGPT), then ask in plain words. Here is a prompt built from the tested example:

My role is not stated. Ask me for my role first. Use IMPACT's Map Alternatives tool. your product: our product; category: cloud security monitoring; competitors: your first competitor, your second competitor; your strengths: exposure-based ranking, one view across three clouds. If this tool is unavailable, ask me to open its web page; do not invent its output. How does the Map Alternatives tool work?

You do not need to sign in. A web browser is enough, and the answer comes back in a second or two.

  1. Fill in the form above, or press Fill in an example.
  2. Press Run Map Alternatives.
  3. Read the result on this page, then copy it and edit it before you use it.

It applies fixed rules in the IMPACT code to the inputs above and returns text. It does not call an AI model, does not look anything up, and does not store what you type. The web form and the AI assistant connector run the same tool code, so the same input gives the same answer in both.
