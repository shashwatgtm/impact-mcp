Champion Hypothesis
Primary Champion (Role hypothesis, unconfirmed)

Most Likely Role: SOC or cloud security lead

Why This Role:

  • Owns the problem: the problem you described, in your words: "too many cloud alerts and slow audit preparation"
  • Personal motivation: a visible win on what this sector measures: time to prepare an audit, alerts per analyst, audit findings, critical exposures open
  • Credibility is unconfirmed until the named contact demonstrates advocacy and access to the decision maker

Champion Validation Questions:

  1. "Who is responsible for time to prepare an audit today, and who answers for it when it slips?"
  2. "Who brought this initiative to leadership's attention?"
  3. "Who would be promoted/recognized if this problem was solved?"
  4. "Who's actively researching solutions in this space?"
Economic Buyer (Budget Authority)

Most Likely Role: CISO

Why This Role:

  • Primary concern: the same measures, and the cost of leaving the problem unsolved. How deals usually run here: CISO led, with a proof of value on the buyer's own environment; budget often follows an audit finding or an incident.
  • Buying trigger: an event that releases budget (a renewal, an audit, a season or a target): ask what the event is

Economic Buyer Discovery Questions:

  1. "Which of these would this move for you: time to prepare an audit, alerts per analyst, audit findings?"
  2. "How does this tie to company strategic priorities?"
  3. "What's the cost of not solving this problem?"
Technical Influencer (Implementation Voice)

Most Likely Role: CIO or CTO

Why This Role:

  • Evaluation criteria: CIO or CTO checks integration
  • Potential blocker: the objections this sector often raises: "We already have a tool for this"; "We have too many alerts already"; "Integration with our SIEM and ticketing"; "We need proof before budget"
Commercial and Control Reviewers

Risk, compliance and audit review the evidence it produces. They rarely champion a purchase, but each can stop one: ask the champion when they join.

Technical Influencer Discovery Questions:

  1. "What would a successful implementation look like?"
  2. "What's failed before and why?"
  3. "What systems does this need to integrate with?"
Daily Users

Security engineers use it every day. Ask them what a good week looks like today; their answer is your first proof point.

Using Your Price Point and Target Companies
  • Price point: you gave "\$3,750 per month". Ask the champion early whether that sits inside a budget they control or needs the economic buyer's sign-off; the answer tells you how many people you must reach.
  • Target companies: you target "mid-size fintech and SaaS companies on two or more clouds". Check how each role above is titled in those companies. In smaller ones one person often holds two of the roles; in larger ones procurement or finance usually joins the committee.
Sector view: cybersecurity, cloud security
  • Words this sector's buyers use: attack surface, exposure, misconfiguration, alert fatigue, mean time to detect, mean time to respond, SOC, compliance audit, risk register, threat intelligence.
  • A proof point that lands: Exposures found and closed during the proof of value, with the time it took to fix them.
  • Discovery questions in this sector's language:
  1. Which assets, systems or environments are in scope, and which are you least sure about?
  2. How many alerts does the team handle in a week, and how are they ranked today?
  3. What did the last audit or incident show?
  4. Which tools must this work with (SIEM, ticketing, cloud accounts)?
  5. How would you judge a proof of value a success?

Champion Development Path

If you're starting without an identified champion:

Week 1: Map the organization

  • Identify 3-5 potential champions based on hypotheses above
  • Research their LinkedIn, recent posts, company news
  • Find mutual connections for warm introductions

Week 2: Multi-thread outreach

  • Contact SOC or cloud security lead with a problem-focused message
  • Contact the reviewers listed above with a solution-focused message
  • See who engages first → likely champion

Week 3: Validate and align

  • Confirm champion's pain matches your value prop
  • Understand their buying process
  • Map the decision-making unit together
Champion Enablement Hypothesis

Once you identify your champion, they'll need:

  1. Internal Business Case: ROI data to share with CISO
  2. Technical Validation: Proof points for the reviewers above
  3. Competitive Comparison: Why not alternatives (status quo, competitors)
  4. Risk Mitigation: Implementation plan, support structure, success metrics

Who is the Identify Champions tool not ideal for?

  • Anyone who cannot give the inputs listed above, because the answer is built only from them.
  • Teams that need buyer research done for them: IMPACT structures your positioning as hypotheses, and you test them with buyers.
  • Anyone who needs live competitor data: the tools do not look anything up. They work from what you type about your market and rivals, and anything they add themselves (such as common complaints in a category) is marked as something to check with buyers.

How does the Identify Champions tool work?

You do not need to sign in. A web browser is enough, and the answer comes back in a second or two.

  1. Fill in the form above, or press Fill in an example.
  2. Press Run Identify Champions.
  3. Read the result on this page, then copy it and edit it before you use it.

It applies fixed rules in the IMPACT code to the inputs above and returns text. It does not call an AI model, does not look anything up, and does not store what you type. The web form and the AI assistant connector run the same tool code, so the same input gives the same answer in both.
